# Technical Writing — Concise Notes

## 1. What is Technical Writing?
A specialized field of communication whose purpose is to convey **technical/scientific information accurately and efficiently**. It deals with subjects in:
- Science
- Engineering
- Technology
- Medicine
- Business, Trade, Government

> Goal: help readers **use** a technology or **understand** a process/concept — not to showcase the writer's voice.

## 2. Categories of Technical Documents
1. **Reports & communications** (day-to-day business — memos, letters, status reports)
2. **Operational manuals** — instructions/procedures
3. **Technical papers, magazines, articles, books, theses** (education/teaching)
4. **Patent documents**

## 3. Key Attributes of Technical Writing
| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Impersonal | No subjective language/opinion |
| Concise | To the point, but detailed |
| Directed | Written for a specific audience |
| Archival | Can be saved, reused, revised |
| Cites others | References related work in the field |
| Specific style/format | Uses charts, graphs, test results |
| Pertains to technical subject | Stays on the relevant topic |

## 4. Why Technical Writers Matter
- Often the **only communication channel** with clients, government, managers, other companies.
- Readers use the document to **perform a task, decide, solve a problem, or gain knowledge**.

## 5. Common Purposes of Technical Writing
1. **To Inform** (primary purpose)
2. **To Instruct**
3. **To Persuade** (analyze/interpret events and implications)

## 6. Skills a Technical Writer Needs
- Subject knowledge
- Writing skills
- Research skills
- Knowledge of technical communication conventions

## 7. Functions of Technical Writing
- Basis for management decisions
- Furnish information / give instructions
- Explain techniques, report achievements
- Analyze problems, determine design/system requirements
- Public relations, stockholder reports
- Develop products, provide service
- Record & pursue business proposals

## 8. History of Technical Writing (brief timeline)
- **Prehistoric** cave paintings (hunting techniques)
- **Babylonians**: clay tablets (astronomy, math, agriculture, beer-making)
- **Ancient Egyptians**: papyrus (medicine, math)
- **Ancient Greeks**: math, science, biology, psychology — foundation of Western thought
- **16th century**: first military weapons manual
- **WWII**: huge boost — need to explain new weapons/devices quickly
- **Today**: needed for tech, consumer products, and services

## 9. Genres of Technical Writing
Instruction manuals · Procedure guidelines · Reports · Specifications · Proposals · CVs/Resumes · Business letters/memos · Research papers · Other technical docs

## 10. Why Writing Skills Matter for Technical Professionals
1. Writing is core to daily work (progress reports, memos, emails, inquiry letters)
2. Enables communication with co-workers, clients, supervisors
3. Essential for career growth — poor writing reflects poorly on the organization
4. Saves time and money (clarity avoids wasted effort)

## 11. Technical vs Academic Writing
| Technical | Academic |
|---|---|
| Practical, job-related | Displays knowledge |
| Informed writer → uninformed reader | Student → teacher |
| Many readers | Usually one reader (teacher) |
| Objective | Can be subjective |

## 12. Technical vs Literary Writing
| Aspect | Technical | Literary |
|---|---|---|
| Rules | Rule-governed, formal structure | Experimental |
| Organization | Highly logical | Can be loose |
| Clarity | Explicit, direct | Can be implicit/covert |
| Tone | Formal, objective | Varies (humorous, poetic, emotional, etc.) |
| Style | Straightforward | Uses metaphors, irony, puns |
| Vocabulary | Standard/formal | Varies by audience |
| Content | Factual, specialized | Wide range of topics |
| Visuals | Tables, graphs, charts | Rarely used |
| Overall | Informative, limited interpretation | Emotional, imaginative, multiple interpretations |

## 13. Academic vs Business/Technical Writing (style comparison)
| Academic | Business/Technical |
|---|---|
| Prose-based | Lists, bullets, short paragraphs |
| Thesis in opening paragraph | Thesis in a phrase/heading |
| Sentence diversity valued | Parallelism/repetition valued |
| Sources = permanent record | Sources relevant only to the message |

## 14. Technical Writing in Computer Science
Key document types across the software lifecycle:
1. **Project Proposal** – introduces project, states the problem & solution
2. **Project Overview Specification (POS)** – overview, goals, schedule, budget, constraints
3. **Software Project Management Plan (SPMP)** – manages process, risks, schedule
4. **Software Requirements Specification (SRS)** – defines functionality, use-cases, data dictionary
5. **Software Design Specification (SDS/SDD)** – system architecture, interface design
6. **System Test Specification/Report (STS/STR)** – test plan & results
7. **User Manuals** – step-by-step instructions for end-users
8. **Code Comments** – explain code behavior for other developers
9. **Memoranda** – short, purpose-driven internal communication
10. **Technical/Evaluation/Recommendation Reports**

**3 keys to good technical writing in CS:**
- Clarity & organization (explain system, its significance, future work)
- Ability to summarize logically
- Mechanics/syntax matter (a compiler won't forgive errors, unlike a human reader)

## 15. Practical Importance of Technical Writing
- Ensures products/processes are used **safely and correctly**
- Required for **patents** and **investor pitches**
- Helps consumers assemble/use products (furniture, appliances, etc.)
- Reduces downtime via manuals/reference docs
- Saves time & money; boosts career prospects

## 16. Job Role Snapshot: Technical Writer
**Responsibilities:** work with teams to understand product; write UI text, help docs, developer guides, tutorials; maintain information architecture.
**Requirements:** writing experience, attention to detail, ability to simplify complex concepts, strong English, MS Office skills, CS/Engineering degree (preferred).

---
### Quick Exam-Style Takeaways
- **Primary purpose** of technical writing = **to inform**.
- Technical writing is **objective**, **precise**, **audience-specific**, and **not exclusive to science/tech** (it appears in business, law, medicine, government too).
- It differs from literary writing mainly in **tone, clarity, and use of stylistic devices**.
- It differs from academic writing mainly in **audience** and **purpose**.
